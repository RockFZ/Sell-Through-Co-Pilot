Fucheng Zhu  fuchengz@usc.edu Abhigna Veerendra Kumar  veerendr@usc.edu Shivani Rao  raoshiva@usc.edu Sell-Through Co-Pilot Plan re-stocking, pricing, and promotions together — fast, explainable, and built on widely used open tools. 1) Problem & why it matters Promotions and ads bring visitors, but if items aren’t available you waste money and disappoint customers; if you over-buy “to be safe,” returns and leftovers burn cash. Teams often choose re-stocking rules, discounts, and ad budgets in separate silos, so campaigns run into run-outs, followed by markdowns and wasted ad spend. We propose a realistic, operational way to plan re-stocking + discounts + ad pacing together that runs fast enough to be used interactively. 2) Prior art we will build on (in-use, accessible) • Meta Robyn (open-source MMM) — from Meta Marketing Science; provides response curves and budget/discount optimization workflows with docs and case studies you can run now. (Facebook Experimental) • Uber Orbit (open-source forecasting) — from Uber; daily Bayesian time-series forecasting with uncertainty, used internally for planning; GitHub + Uber blog. (GitHub) • Odoo Inventory (open-source ERP) — production “Reordering Rules” and Replenishment scheduler to propose purchase orders back to target; full user docs. (Odoo) • ERPNext (open-source ERP) — Auto Reorder: when stock drops below a configured level, it auto-creates a Material Request for purchase/transfer/production. (Frappe ⽂档) (External validation in public health): OpenBoxes “Reorder Report” and OpenLMIS deployments document min/reorder/max workflows and reduced stock-out rates. (OpenBoxes知识库) 3) What we will build (high level) 3.1 Ground-truth simulation layer Use Orbit to forecast baseline demand (mean + uncertainty). Use Robyn to produce promo/ads lift curves and “what-if” scenarios for discount & spend. Feed these into a slow but accurate inventory play-out (lead-time noise, returns). In parallel, script Odoo (Reordering Rules / Replenishment) and ERPNext (Auto Reorder → Material Request) to capture their native re-stocking proposals under the same scenarios; log all outcomes. (Uber) 
3.2 Fast surrogate on GPU Train a small tabular surrogate (e.g., XGBoost with gpu_hist) to predict: money made, on-time delivery, leftover units, and ad-waste, from product traits, delivery-time stats, re-stocking rule, discount, and channel budgets. XGBoost’s GPU docs and treemethod references support this directly. (XGBoost ⽂档) 3.3 Scaled search + “trust-but-verify” Use a small Ray/Dask cluster to (a) generate many candidate plans, (b) score with the surrogate in parallel, and (c) re-simulate only finalists with the slow engine. Ray’s scaling guides show these patterns. (Ray) 4) Inputs, decisions, outputs Inputs Products/variants; unit cost & price; daily sales history; expected returns & time-to-return; supplier lead-time stats; promo calendar and candidate discounts; planned ad traffic by channel; practical limits (smallest order allowed, case pack, weekly cash cap, receiving capacity). Decisions we recommend • Re-stocking targets/rules per product (simple, explainable rules such as min–max or order-up-to, aligned with Odoo/ERPNext). (Odoo) • Promo/discount level that meets the sales goal without causing run-outs or creating large leftovers (via Robyn what-ifs). (Facebook Experimental) • Ad pacing (turn spend down when run-out risk is high; lean in when coverage is safe). Outputs for leaders • Promo readiness light (green / yellow / red). • Suggested re-stocking, discount, and ad level. • Money view: projected sales, ad spend avoided on unavailable items, leftover value avoided. • Customer view: on-time delivery & risk of running out. • Ops view: inbound loads vs dock capacity; weekly cash usage. 5) Methods (concrete) Forecasting & promo effects (traditional) Fit Orbit at daily cadence; back-test. Fit Robyn on sales/spend/discount to learn channel response & saturation; export response curves and budget splits for target windows (e.g., promo week). (GitHub) 
Inventory play-out (traditional) For each joint plan, simulate day-by-day demand = Orbit baseline + Robyn lift; apply lead-time draws, a simple re-stocking rule, returns, and constraints (smallest order, case pack, cash, receiving). In parallel, run Odoo Replenishment and ERPNext Auto Reorder to capture their purchase suggestions; store all outcomes as labeled rows. (Odoo) Surrogate learning (AI model) Train XGBoost-GPU to map (features + plan) → (cost, on-time rate, leftovers, ad waste). Use early stopping and, where helpful, monotonicity constraints; report feature importance for explainability. (XGBoost ⽂档) Scaled search (systems) Use Ray to parallelize plan generation and surrogate scoring; re-simulate only the top-K per product group/campaign for accuracy. (Ray) 6) Evaluation plan (three scenarios) 1. Normal week — compare baseline rules vs Co-Pilot plan. 2. Big weekend promo — slide discount & ad traffic; show Co-Pilot raises pre-build just enough, keeps run-out risk low, avoids leftover spike. 3. Influencer spike — toggle “viral risk”; Co-Pilot lifts safety buffers, splits purchase orders, trims ads/discount to survive the surge. Reported metrics Promo revenue ↑ with run-outs ↓; ad waste ↓ (fewer clicks to items that can’t ship); leftovers & tied-up cash ↓; planning time ↓ (surrogate vs slow sim); ablations for lead-time variance and returns. 7) Milestones (5 weeks) • W1–2 — Data plumbing; Orbit + Robyn on a small example; Odoo/ERPNext stood up for 3–5 products; first slow simulations logged. (Uber) • W3 — Surrogate trained on GPU; validate on hold-out plans; basic dashboard (readiness light + risk timeline). (XGBoost ⽂档) • W4 — Distributed search with Ray; trust-but-verify loop; influencer-spike switch. (Ray) • W5 — Final evaluation on three scenarios; short video demo; write-up. 8) Risks & mitigations • Helper bias near tight constraints → Always re-simulate finalists; add penalties for risky edges. 
• Data gaps for returns or ad forecasts → Start with simple defaults; improve as signals arrive. • Seasonality drift → Re-fit Orbit/Robyn monthly; re-train surrogate weekly on recent simulations. 9) References (accessible, being-used components) • Robyn overview & docs; Meta’s open MMM package.
